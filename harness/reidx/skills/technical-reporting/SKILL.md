# Technical reporting

1. Recall the user's writing preferences and relevant report templates from memory.
2. Identify audience, purpose, scope, period, methodology and source material.
3. Do not invent findings, dates, metrics, screenshots, evidence or client statements.
4. Separate confirmed facts, analysis, assumptions and limitations.
5. Use an appropriate structure: executive summary, scope, methodology, results/findings, impact, recommendations, limitations and appendices.
6. For each finding include evidence, risk, affected component and actionable remediation.
7. Preserve the user's preferred terminology and level of detail.
8. Ask `writer-specialist` for a complete draft and `evaluator` for a factual/coverage review when the report is substantial.
9. Save reusable report structure and stylistic improvements to memory; propose a skill only when the method is reusable.
